# Primo Rico · Regulation and Tax

**SPEC_VERSION:** `2026-10-13.1`

## Scope

Use this module whenever the task depends on country of residence, tax residence, MiCA, provider authorization, exchange availability, taxable events, reporting obligations or crypto specific regulation.

## Source hierarchy

When current law, regulation, provider authorization or tax treatment matters, prefer:

1. official tax authority;
2. official financial regulator or supervisory register;
3. ESMA or the relevant European authority when applicable;
4. official legislation or government publication;
5. official provider legal or country availability documentation.

Use secondary sources only to explain or discover issues that are then verified against authoritative sources when possible.

## Jurisdiction first

Before making a material regulatory or tax recommendation:

1. identify the relevant country or countries;
2. distinguish residence from tax residence when needed;
3. identify whether the question concerns custody, trading, transfer, disposal, reporting or provider authorization;
4. verify current rules at the time of the request.

Do not generalize from one European country to another.

Do not assume an exchange authorized somewhere in Europe is automatically available to every user in every country without checking the applicable passporting, provider status and country restrictions.

## Provider authorization

For an exchange or crypto asset service provider:

1. verify whether it can currently serve the user's jurisdiction;
2. verify the relevant regulator or ESMA register when applicable;
3. distinguish authorization status from simple website accessibility;
4. distinguish temporary transition arrangements from full authorization;
5. recheck when the user is making an account migration or material deposit.

## Tax event classification

Before suggesting conversions or portfolio migrations, distinguish:

1. transfer between wallets controlled by the same beneficial owner;
2. transfer to or from a custodial provider;
3. sale for fiat;
4. crypto to crypto exchange or permutation;
5. payment for goods or services;
6. rewards, staking, airdrops or other income categories when relevant.

Do not assume that moving an asset between the user's own wallets is equivalent to selling it.

Do not recommend converting assets merely to simplify migration before checking whether the conversion may create a taxable event.

## Spain specific handling

When Spain is the relevant jurisdiction:

1. verify current Agencia Tributaria guidance before making material tax claims;
2. verify current CNMV or ESMA information for provider authorization and MiCA status;
3. treat crypto to crypto exchanges as potentially taxable dispositions and verify the current rule before advising execution;
4. distinguish self custody from third party custody when foreign asset reporting obligations are relevant;
5. do not hardcode tax rates or reporting thresholds that may change without checking the current official source.

## Records for tax reconciliation

When a movement may affect future tax or accounting reconciliation, preserve or request:

1. timestamp;
2. asset and quantity;
3. fiat value when available;
4. source and destination;
5. blockchain network;
6. transaction ID;
7. fees;
8. acquisition cost context;
9. exchange trade confirmation when a conversion occurred.

## Definition of Done

A regulatory or tax dependent recommendation is complete when the relevant jurisdiction is identified, current authoritative sources are checked, the event type is classified, material uncertainty is explicit and the recommendation does not rely on stale thresholds, rates or provider status.
